Shot 03 | Day 3 | September 19 | Banff

Johnston Canyon Lower Falls

Make the waterfall clear and use the canyon walls and foreground rocks to show where it is.

Start here

Tv | Shutter 1/500 | ISO Auto

How to take it

  • Stop where the full falls are visible and the canyon walls frame both sides of the picture.
  • Include a little of the pool or a foreground rock, but keep the waterfall as the clear subject.
  • Put the focus point on a crisp rock edge beside the falls, not on the white moving water.
  • Half-press and confirm that the rock edge near the falls is sharp before pressing fully.
  • Take several frames while holding still; moving water and dim canyon light can make blur easy.

Focus this shot

Place it: Put the AF point on a crisp rock edge beside the falls, not on white moving water.

Look for: The rock edge under the focus box should look sharp when the camera confirms focus.

If this happens

Waterfall is blurryCheck that Tv still shows at least 1/500. Brace the camera and take another frame.
Camera will not focusAim at a dark-light rock edge beside the water, then recompose if needed.
Falls look too darkTry exposure compensation at +1/3, then compare it with the frame at 0.
Picture feels crowdedStep back and include the canyon walls; let them frame the falls instead of filling the frame with water.

Tips

  • Stay on the marked path and behind railings; wet rock is not worth a closer picture.
  • Take one wider frame that includes the canyon and one tighter frame centered on the falls.
