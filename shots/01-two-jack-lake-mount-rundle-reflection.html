Shot 01 | Day 3 | September 19 | Banff

Two Jack Lake - Mount Rundle Reflection

Make Mount Rundle and its reflection feel like one complete shape. Keep the camera level and give the reflection enough room below the shoreline.

Start here

Av | Aperture f/8 | ISO Auto

How to take it

  • Stand where the mountain and its reflection are both clear, then hold the camera level.
  • Place the shoreline near the middle only when the reflection is strong; otherwise give the mountain more room than the water.
  • Put the focus point on a crisp, high-contrast edge of Mount Rundle—not on the shore, water, or sky.
  • Half-press the shutter and wait for the focus box to confirm on the mountain before pressing fully.
  • Check that the mountain is sharp, then take a second frame in case the water becomes calmer.

Focus this shot

Place it: Put the AF point on a high-contrast edge of Mount Rundle, not on the nearby shore, water, or sky.

Look for: The focus box should settle on the mountain and turn green or otherwise confirm focus before you press fully.

If this happens

Wrong thing is sharpMove the focus point onto a crisp edge of Mount Rundle and refocus.
Camera will not focusAim at a bright-dark mountain edge; avoid smooth water and open sky.
Reflection looks paleLower the camera slightly and wait briefly for calmer water.
Whole picture is blurryCheck the shutter speed. Brace the camera and retake if it is below 1/125.

Tips

  • Take both a balanced reflection frame and a second frame with slightly more mountain than water.
  • Keep the horizon straight; a tilted shoreline is especially noticeable in a reflection.
