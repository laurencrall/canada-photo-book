Athabasca Falls - Freeze the Water

Show the falls clearly with enough surrounding rock that it reads as a powerful waterfall scene.

Start here

Tv | Shutter 1/500 | ISO Auto

How to take it

  • Choose a viewpoint where the main rush of water and rock edges are both visible.
  • Keep the waterfall as the subject; use rock only to frame it.
  • Put the AF point on a crisp rock edge beside the water.
  • Half-press and confirm the rock edge is sharp.
  • Take several steady frames; the fast water and dim light make blur easy.

Focus this shot

Place it: Put the AF point on a crisp rock edge beside the water.

Look for: The rock edge should look crisp when the camera confirms focus.

If this happens

Water is blurryMake sure Tv shows 1/500 or faster; brace and retake.
Camera will not focusAim at a dark-light rock edge beside the water.
Falls look too darkTry +1/3 exposure compensation and compare.
Picture feels crowdedStep back and let a clear rock edge frame the falls.

Tips

  • Stay on marked paths and behind railings; wet rock is not worth a closer picture.
  • Show the main falls, not just a patch of white water.
