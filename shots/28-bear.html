Bear

The first job is safety: never move closer for a bear photo. A distant bear in its landscape is the successful picture.

Start here

Tv | Shutter 1/500 | ISO Auto

How to take it

  • Stay with your group, keep a very large distance, and follow park guidance.
  • Do not approach, feed, block, or follow the bear.
  • From a safe place, use the longest zoom and put the AF point on the head.
  • Half-press and take a few frames without changing the animal's behavior.
  • Leave immediately if the bear notices you or changes behavior.

Focus this shot

Place it: From a safe distance, put the AF point on the bear's head.

Look for: The head should look crisp when focus confirms.

If this happens

Bear is too smallKeep the landscape version or crop later. Never move closer.
Bear looks toward youStop taking photos, back away calmly, and follow park guidance.
Bear is blurryUse 1/1000 if it is walking or moving.
No safe viewSkip the photo. Safety and distance come first.

Tips

  • A bear photo never justifies getting closer, leaving a vehicle, or leaving a safe path.
  • Use the 70mm as an environmental lens; crop later rather than closing distance.
