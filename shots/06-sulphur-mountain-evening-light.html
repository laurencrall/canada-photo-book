Shot 06 | Day 5 | September 21 | Banff

Sulphur Mountain - Evening Light

Use the warm evening light to show the shape of the ridges. Protect the bright sky, and keep a recognizable mountain edge as the main subject.

Start here

Av | Aperture f/8 | ISO Auto

How to take it

  • Choose one clear ridge or peak as the subject instead of trying to include every direction at once.
  • Keep the brightest sky away from the exact center and include enough darker land to show depth.
  • Put the focus point on a crisp ridge edge where light and dark areas meet—not on the open sky.
  • Half-press the shutter and make sure the ridge under the focus box looks crisp before pressing fully.
  • Take a normal frame, then a slightly darker frame while the light is changing.

Focus this shot

Place it: Put the AF point on the edge of the main ridge where light and dark areas meet. Do not focus on open sky.

Look for: The ridge under the focus box should look crisp when the camera confirms focus.

If this happens

Wrong thing is sharpMove the focus point off nearby people or railings and onto the distant ridge.
Camera will not focusUse a bright-dark ridge edge; avoid open sky or a featureless shadow.
Sky is whiteSet exposure compensation to -1/3; if needed, try -2/3.
Picture is blurryBrace against something solid. If the speed is below 1/125, raise ISO.

Tips

  • Evening color can change quickly, so take several frames without moving the composition.
  • Take a normal frame, then a slightly darker frame while the light is changing.
