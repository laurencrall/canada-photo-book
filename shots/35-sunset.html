Sunset

Give the sky the room it deserves, but anchor it with one dark, simple foreground shape so it does not become an empty color wash.

Start here

Av | Aperture f/8 | ISO Auto

How to take it

  • Choose a clear foreground shape: mountain, tree, shoreline, or person.
  • Keep the horizon level and place it low enough to show the sky.
  • Put the AF point on the foreground edge or a distant crisp silhouette.
  • Half-press and confirm focus, then take one frame at 0 and one darker.
  • Keep taking pictures as the colors change after the sun disappears.

Focus this shot

Place it: Put the AF point on a foreground edge or distant crisp silhouette.

Look for: The selected edge should look crisp when focus confirms.

If this happens

Sky is washed outTry -1/3 or -2/3 exposure compensation.
Foreground is emptyAdd one dark simple shape to anchor the frame.
Horizon is tiltedStraighten the camera before another frame.
Color fadesKeep shooting after sunset; color often improves briefly.

Tips

  • The best color is often after the sun has gone below the horizon.
  • Do not stop at one frame; small exposure changes matter at sunset.
